Understanding Storms, Floods, and Typhoon

A typhoon is a powerful tropical cyclone characterized by strong winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surges. In the Philippines and Western Pacific region, these storms are called typhoons, while they are known as hurricanes in the Atlantic and Eastern Pacific. Storms form over warm ocean waters and can cause devastating damage through high winds, flooding, landslides, and coastal storm surges. Understanding wind signals and proper preparation can save lives and reduce damage to property.

Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS)

1

Signal #1

Winds: 39–61 km/h. Minimal to minor threat. Be alert and monitor weather updates. Secure light outdoor objects and avoid unnecessary travel.

2

Signal #2

Winds: 62–88 km/h. Minor to moderate threat. Secure outdoor items, prepare emergency supplies, and avoid coastal areas during high waves.

3

Signal #3

Winds: 89–117 km/h. Moderate to significant threat. Stay indoors, expect widespread damage to structures, trees, and power lines. Follow evacuation orders if issued.

4

Signal #4

Winds: 118–184 km/h. Significant to severe threat. Very dangerous conditions expected. Take immediate shelter in a safe location away from windows; prepare for extended power outages and infrastructure damage.

5

Signal #5

Winds: 185 km/h or higher. Extreme threat to life and property. Catastrophic damage is likely. Seek immediate shelter in the strongest part of your home or an evacuation center, and follow all instructions from authorities.

Understanding Flood Causes

Heavy Rainfall

Prolonged or intense rainfall can overwhelm drainage systems and cause rivers to overflow, leading to widespread flooding in urban and rural areas.

Clogged Waterways

Garbage, debris, and sediment block drainage systems and waterways, preventing proper water flow and causing water to accumulate and flood surrounding areas.

Low-lying Areas

Areas located below sea level or near bodies of water are naturally more susceptible to flooding, especially during severe weather and high tides.

Storm & Flood Safety Guide

BEFORE A STORM OR FLOOD

  1. Monitor weather forecasts and warnings from PAGASA regularly.
  2. Prepare an emergency kit with food, water, medicines, and important documents.
  3. Identify evacuation routes and nearest evacuation centers.

BEFORE A STORM OR FLOOD

  1. Secure outdoor furniture, equipment, and loose objects that could become projectiles.
  2. Clear gutters and drainage systems around your property to reduce the risk of flooding.
  3. Charge all electronic devices and prepare backup power sources.
  4. Store drinking water in clean containers and keep emergency supplies accessible.

DURING A STORM OR FLOOD

  1. Stay indoors and away from windows and glass doors.
  2. Never attempt to walk or drive through floodwaters — even shallow water can be dangerous.
  3. Turn off electricity at the main breaker if flooding is imminent and it is safe to do so.

DURING A STORM OR FLOOD

  1. Move to higher ground immediately if water starts entering your home.
  2. Listen to battery-powered radio or official channels for emergency updates.
  3. Evacuate immediately if ordered by authorities and follow designated routes.
  4. Avoid using candles; use flashlights to prevent fire hazards.

Items to Secure at Home

  • Outdoor furniture, grills, and decorations — bring indoors or secure them.
  • Garbage bins and recycling containers — empty and store safely.
  • Garden tools, ladders, and loose equipment — store in a safe place.
  • Vehicles — park in covered or secure areas if possible.
  • Loose roofing materials and gutters — reinforce or secure to prevent damage.
  • Windows and doors — board up or protect if advised by authorities.
  • Important documents — place in waterproof containers or a secure, elevated location.
